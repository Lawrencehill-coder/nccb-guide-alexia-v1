Getting Oriented
Purpose of This Guide

This is a working reference to help Alexia conduct interviews for NCCB Credit candidates — roles such as Credit Specialist that involve direct client calls under our Texas OCCC Chapter 394 debt management license.

It is intentionally a working draft. Once we've run a few interviews together and refined the questions and scoring, we'll fold this into a formal NCCB Interview Manual and SOP.

A working draft, refined together — not a finished manual.
Why it matters: Getting this right protects both the candidate's experience and NCCB's compliance standing from day one.
02
Before You Sit Down
Prep Checklist
  • Confirm the specific role and whether it is client-facing (calls) or back-office (payroll, admin, support).
  • Review the candidate's resume for any prior collections, credit counseling, or licensed financial services experience.
  • Pull 2–3 real (anonymized) call scenarios to use as prompts — recent coaching notes are a good source of realistic situations.
  • Block enough time for a full structured interview plus notes immediately after — don't rely on memory later.
Habit to build: Do this prep the same way every time. Consistency going in is what makes candidates comparable coming out.
03
The Format
Structuring the Interview

Run the interview in four phases. Keep it conversational, but don't skip a phase — consistency across candidates is what makes comparisons fair.

  1. Opening (5 min): Put the candidate at ease, explain the role and what a typical day looks like.
  2. Core competency questions (15–20 min): Use the framework in Section 04.
  3. Scenario walk-through (10–15 min): Give the candidate a realistic client call scenario and have them respond out loud.
  4. Candidate questions & close (5–10 min): Let them ask questions; tell them the next step and a realistic timeline.
Four phases, every time — that's what makes candidates comparable.
04
Five Dimensions
Evaluation Framework

NCCB adapts the standard five-dimension hiring framework — competence, confidence, communication, character, and culture/chemistry — to our specific compliance and client-handling environment.

CompetenceCan learn and correctly execute the counseling and enrollment process, including Texas OCCC Chapter 394 requirements.Ask them to explain a fee-based service to a skeptical client.
ConfidenceStays on script and holds a firm, professional close without becoming passive when a client pushes back.Ask for an example of holding their ground with a resistant customer.

05
What Actually Separates Hires
Compliance & Process Awareness
Fee sequence discipline: $140.95 counseling fee → $39.95 My800Scores fee → $50 per account. Ask how they'd make sure they never skip a step.
Accurate escalation summaries: Ask the candidate to summarize a scenario back to you — watch for a cleaned-up version versus what actually happened.
Closing with commitment, not passivity: Present a scenario where a client hesitates — do they ask for a clear next step, or let it trail off?
Recognizing client urgency: Bury a time-sensitive detail in a scenario and see if they catch it unprompted.
06
Reading the Ramp-Up
The 30-60-90 Day Plan

For roles where the candidate will need to ramp up on calls and compliance quickly, it's fair to ask how they'd approach their first 90 days — even informally.

  • Days 1–30: learning the process, shadowing calls, getting comfortable with the fee sequence and scripts.
  • Days 31–60: taking calls independently, applying feedback from coaching.
  • Days 61–90: consistent, compliant calls with minimal correction needed.
Instant mastery isn't the goal — a realistic ramp-up is.
07
Worth a Second Look
Red Flags
  • Can't give a specific example when asked about a past mistake or piece of feedback — everything is described in the abstract.
  • Downplays the importance of following a required process step-by-step "if the client doesn't mind."
  • Gets defensive or dismissive when you push back gently during the scenario walk-through.
  • Talks over or rushes the client in role-play scenarios rather than confirming understanding.
08
Follow-Through
After the Interview
  • Write up notes within the same day, scored against the five dimensions in Section 04 — don't wait, details fade fast.
  • Compare notes with Lawrence before extending or declining an offer.
  • Give the candidate a realistic timeline for next steps, and follow it — candidates remember how they were treated in the process.
Candidates remember how they were treated — win or lose.
